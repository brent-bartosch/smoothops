# Candidate-side optimization for AI-driven recruiter search and screening (2026)

Scope: what LinkedIn profile and personal-website signals LLM-based sourcing/screening systems reward or penalize, as of October 2026. Audience: US-based senior GTM engineer / RevOps builder / solo consultant wanting both recruiter inbound and client inbound.

Evidence legend used throughout:
- **[VERIFIED]** = LinkedIn/vendor/Google primary documentation, peer-reviewed or engineering-blog descriptions of production systems, or controlled/large-sample studies.
- **[PRACTITIONER]** = experienced recruiter/sourcer/writer opinion, anecdotes, small samples, vendor blogs.
- **[DATED]** = source is pre-2025; may not reflect current systems.

## Key Question 1: Semantic/LLM search vs Boolean — does exact-title keyword text still matter, or does descriptive outcome-rich prose now get matched?

### Takeaway
Both now matter, in different layers. LinkedIn has publicly documented (June 2026 engineering blog, Feb 2026 arXiv paper) that Recruiter and Hiring Assistant now use LLM-distilled embedding retrieval plus LLM cross-encoder reranking, where "the same qualification can be described in dozens of ways" and relevance is scored on title, seniority, industry, hard skills, and education — so descriptive prose about what you actually did is now retrievable. But keyword/Boolean search is still the default mode in Recruiter, human recruiters still scan the headline/current title in seconds, and LinkedIn's own help docs still tell members to use standard job titles. The safe strategy is: conventional, searchable titles in the structured fields, plus specific outcome-rich prose in About/Experience for the semantic layer.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant semantic search engineering post (Zhiltsov, Ramesh Bapu, Thakur, Liu; published June 11, 2026) states that before semantic search, "boolean (keyword) search offered better precision but returned zero results for nearly half of queries," and that "the same qualification can be described in dozens of ways." The relevance policy: "Qualification matches are based strictly on experience, skills, and role fit," later expanded to "a multi-dimensional score covering title, seniority, industry, hard skills, and education." The profile encoder "takes the member's profile/resume text and structured attributes and produces a profile embedding." Two-week A/B: "post-LLM Guard HRR +2.7%, InMail Sends per seat +4.1%, Eval Pass Rate trending +3.8%"; candidates sourced per seat down ~4% ("fewer but better"). — [LinkedIn Engineering Blog](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ai/semantic-search-for-ai-agents-at-scale-retrieval-and-ranking-for-linkedins-hiring-assistant); summarized with same numbers by [ZenML LLMOps database](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/semantic-search-for-ai-agents-at-scale-retrieval-and-ranking-for-linkedins-hiring-assistant)
- **[VERIFIED]** Same post: the MUSE (Member Understanding Semantic Embeddings) dual-tower model encodes queries and profiles into one space; Matryoshka training pushes coarse attributes (title, seniority, location) into lower embedding dimensions with finer qualification reasoning in higher dimensions; "structured attributes are the most consistently represented in training data." An "Expert Judge" LLM is the reference implementation of a "constitutional" relevance definition. — [ZenML summary of LinkedIn post](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/semantic-search-for-ai-agents-at-scale-retrieval-and-ranking-for-linkedins-hiring-assistant)
- **[VERIFIED]** arXiv 2602.07309 "Semantic Search At LinkedIn" (Borisyuk et al., ~60 LinkedIn authors, submitted Feb 7, 2026) covers both Job and People Search. "Keyword-based retrieval struggles to capture natural language intent"; semantic search "retrieves and ranks results by meaning rather than token overlap." For People Search "we use summarized profiles to represent both searchers and documents," built from "profiles, professional content, search activity, and historical queries." The 8B LLM judge outputs "graded (0-4) relevance scores with natural-language rationales." The engagement model uses "member text features (e.g., headlines, past positions, locations)." People Search reported >10% NDCG@10 improvement; retrieval P@10 rose 0.33 → 0.47. — [arXiv 2602.07309](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07309v1)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn's AI-Assisted Search product page: "Go beyond standard filters or keywords on profiles to represent skills more accurately"; matches "harder-to-define skills not explicitly listed on resumes"; example qualification "has experience solving ambiguous problems." Recruiters can "copy and paste your intake notes or job description and Recruiter will interpret what you mean." Claimed "+18% higher InMail acceptance rate when compared to search sessions with manual filters" (footnote: "According to LinkedIn data," no methodology). Boolean still fully supported: "If you want to perform a Boolean search without any AI assistance, add your Boolean string... you'll be given the option to run a traditional keyword search." Users can set AI or keyword search as default under AI Preferences. — [LinkedIn Talent Solutions](https://business.linkedin.com/talent-solutions/ai-assisted-search-and-projects)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn AI transparency page: Advanced AI-Assisted Search "will identify which qualifications were found in a candidate's profile and which are missing"; examples of matched qualifications include "Data-driven decision maker" and "excellent written and communication skills." Hiring Assistant "transforms the end user's hiring qualifications into several search queries, evaluates candidates' profiles to access how well they align with the role's qualifications, then retrieves & ranks candidates using LinkedIn Recruiter's standard ranking mechanisms." — [LinkedIn AI Transparency / Hire](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/ai-transparency/hire)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn Help (member-facing, "Order of your profile in people search results") recommends standard job titles because "they are what other members search for," and says "Skills are among the most common queries performed by recruiters and hiring managers." — [LinkedIn Help a521944](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn spokesperson on Hiring Assistant (Euronews, Oct 29, 2024): the agent is designed to "find people based on their skills as opposed to traditional proxies like where someone worked or went to school," and can "understand and evaluate the evidence behind a skill by looking at both explicit and implicit skills." Candidate tips given: keep profile updated "especially when it comes to updating it with skills and experiences"; summarise top skills in About; "connect your skills to the work you've done"; demonstrate proficiency with credentials. — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/10/29/linkedin-has-unveiled-a-new-ai-agent-and-ai-job-coaching-tool-heres-how-to-boost-your-cv-f)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** SocialTalent summary of podcast with LinkedIn CPO Hari Srinivasan: recruiters now see "specific proof points" such as a patent, a PhD, or a project instead of scanning for titles; learning objective "Evaluate candidates by matched skills evidence instead of job title assumptions." (Summary text, not direct quote; undated, ©2026.) — [SocialTalent](https://www.socialtalent.com/podcasts/bonus-episode-linkedins-hari-srinivasan-on-ai-hiring-assistant-the-future-of-recruiting)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Kristof Schoenaerts (retained life-sciences headhunter, uses Recruiter daily), "LinkedIn Optimization: The Headline," Aug 15, 2026: "A creative title makes you invisible to every Boolean search running in your market right now." In Recruiter, "Growth Architect" returns 312 results vs "VP Sales" 1.2 million. "LinkedIn Recruiter does not reliably equate 'VP' with 'Vice President.'" Claims "Head of" and "Lead" don't reliably register in seniority filters; recommends Manager / Director / Vice President / Chief. "The headline makes the promise. The most recent experience entry has to keep it." Dataset of 1,750 exec profiles; says 60-70% include at least one title no recruiter would search for. Methodology not described; partially paywalled. — [Job Search Unlocked Substack](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/linkedin-optimization-the-headline)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** r/recruiting thread "Is Boolean becoming a lost art" (Feb 2025, via mirror): a recruiter using an in-house AI matching tool said it "essentially writes all the AND/OR strings and ranks the skills... really good in theory but has very limited application" and "not even close to living up to leadership's claims yet"; another argued "knowing how to get around AI logic can also be a competitive advantage if the 'AI' solutions are returning the same candidates to every other recruiter." — [r/recruiting mirror](https://cal1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/recruiting/comments/1iosqrb/is_boolean_becoming_a_lost_art)
- **[PRACTITIONER][DATED]** Glen Cathey's older deck on AI/semantic matching: "AI and semantic search apps favor keyword rich resumes and profiles, while keyword poor resumes and profiles may in fact represent better talent"; "People from different companies using the same solution will both find and miss the same people." Pre-LLM era; no 2026 Cathey commentary on LinkedIn's current AI search was found. — [SlideShare](https://www.slideshare.net/glencathey/talent-sourcing-and-matching-artificial-intelligence-and-black-box-semantic-search-vs-human-cognition-and-sourcing)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** ERE webinar framing (2025-26): "Boolean search remains the backbone of effective sourcing, even as AI tools reshape how recruiters find and qualify candidates." — [ERE Pro](https://pro.eremedia.com/videos/boolean-unleashed-smart-search-strategies-in-the-age-of-ai)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Lars Schmidt first look at LinkedIn AI search (June 21, 2024; disclosed paid LinkedIn collaboration): AI "suggests related skills and keywords the recruiter may not have thought of" and converts broad requirements to filters. No failure cases reported; single anecdote. — [Redefining Work](https://redefiningwork.substack.com/p/linkedin-ai-search-first-look)

### Inferences
- The two retrieval paths coexist: Boolean/keyword (still default in Recruiter, exact-string sensitive, title-driven) and semantic (embedding + LLM rerank, qualification-driven). A profile needs to survive both: conventional title strings for the first, concrete described qualifications for the second.
- Because the semantic judge scores "title, seniority, industry, hard skills, education," a profile that states seniority plainly (Director/VP/Head), names the industry/sub-sector, and lists hard skills explicitly will land in the "coarse" embedding dimensions correctly; outcome prose adds the fine-grained qualification match.
- LinkedIn's example qualifications ("experience solving ambiguous problems," "data-driven decision maker") imply the LLM is reading experience narrative, not just skill tags. Prose that shows those behaviors with specifics is now matchable text, which was not true under Boolean.
- Schoenaerts' VP vs Vice President claim is one practitioner's test and may not hold for the semantic layer, but it costs nothing to spell titles out.

### Gaps
- No public documentation of exactly which LinkedIn profile fields (headline vs About vs experience descriptions vs Skills) are concatenated into the MUSE / People Search profile embedding, or their relative weight.
- No controlled candidate-side experiment found (e.g., A/B of two profiles under AI-assisted search). LinkedIn's help explicitly warns that testing with a few accounts "is not likely to reflect the overall order."
- No 2026 statements from Glen Cathey, Jan Tegze, Steve Levy, or Shally Steckerl specifically on candidate-side optimization for LinkedIn's LLM search were surfaced by search. Irina Shamaeva retired from sourcing in Dec 2024 ([Boolean Strings](https://booleanstrings.com/2024/12/16/next-chapter/)).

## Key Question 2: Which LinkedIn profile fields carry the most weight (LinkedIn's own AI search and third-party tools)?

### Takeaway
LinkedIn does not publish field weights, and says search order is personalized per searcher. What LinkedIn does document: skills are among the most common recruiter queries, standard titles are what people search for, profile completeness (positions, schools) helps matching, closer connection degree helps, and keyword stuffing can trigger spam filtering. Practitioner consensus (not verified) is that headline and current title dominate the human and Boolean layers; LinkedIn's semantic layer explicitly scores title, seniority, industry, hard skills, education.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn Help "LinkedIn Search relevance for people search": LinkedIn uses "proprietary relevance algorithms" producing an order "unique to each member"; factors are the searcher's activity, profile, connections and network, profiles that match the query, patterns from members who ran similar searches, and the searcher's history. Advice: "Use keywords that accurately reflect your expertise and experience. Adding more keywords doesn't automatically improve your ranking." "An overly optimized profile may be flagged by spam detection." Testing with a few accounts is unreliable. — [LinkedIn Help a524188](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a524188/)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn Help "Order of your profile in people search results": "can't guarantee a particular order"; "Profile completeness not only helps you show up in more searches" — list current and past positions, schools, fields of study; "Skills are among the most common queries performed by recruiters and hiring managers"; use standard job titles; "The more connections you have, the more likely you will have a connection to the searcher" (2nd-degree beats 3rd); LinkedIn "may take action on accounts that engage in abusive search engine optimization." — [LinkedIn Help a521944](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944)
- **[VERIFIED]** Hiring Assistant relevance dimensions: "title, seniority, industry, hard skills, and education"; profile embedding from "profile/resume text and structured attributes." — [LinkedIn Engineering Blog](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ai/semantic-search-for-ai-agents-at-scale-retrieval-and-ranking-for-linkedins-hiring-assistant)
- **[VERIFIED]** Hiring Assistant with ATS integration (RSC+) evaluates "using both candidate profiles and ATS resume insights"; it "learns from recruiter actions – like which candidates are viewed, contacted, or advanced," confined to that user and role; "does not evaluate based on characteristics such as but not limited to name, age, gender identity." — [LinkedIn AI Transparency](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/ai-transparency/hire)
- **[VERIFIED]** Recruiter "Spotlights" let a recruiter one-click filter results to Open to Work candidates, people with company connections, or people who engaged with the company's content; "Recommended Matches is powered by your real-time hiring signals, such as Save, Hide, or Message candidates." — [LinkedIn: How to use Recruiter](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/resources/talent-acquisition/how-to-use-linkedin-recruiter); [Recruiter Help a419131](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a419131/view-candidates-who-are-open-to-work-in-recruiter)
- **[VERIFIED]** Recruiter keyword search drops stop words: "Adding 'after sales' in the Keywords filter field will return profiles that contain only 'sales'." "+"/"-" operators not supported; use AND/NOT. — [LinkedIn Recruiter Help: Boolean](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Schoenaerts: before clicking, a recruiter sees four things — headline, current title and company, recent roles, location; "you got 3 seconds." Recommends headline formula "Job Title | Geographic Scope | Industry | Sub-Sector | Key Differentiator," fill at least 130 of 220 characters, drop "Ex-" prefixes, avoid double titles ("Sales & Marketing Director" → "Sales Director | Marketing Director"). Claims headline is the most heavily weighted field, with no source. — [Job Search Unlocked](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/linkedin-optimization-the-headline)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** University of Colorado HR guidance: "most recruiting professionals exclusively use the current title box to search for candidates." — [colorado.edu](https://www.colorado.edu/hr/media/2177)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Claims circulating in 2026 career blogs that "headline and current position carry ~60% of search ranking weight" cite a "LinkedIn 2026 Recruiter Search Guide" that could not be located; treat as unsupported. Claims that LinkedIn indexes "first 300 characters of About most heavily" also unsupported. — e.g., [work-club.com checklist](https://work-club.com/linkedin-profile-recruiter-checklist-2026/), [Simplify](https://simplify.jobs/blog/linkedin-profile-optimization-2026)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Third-party sourcing tools and their data: SeekOut's help center describes "conversational search allows you to ask SeekOut Assist to find candidates as if you were having a conversation with a coworker," with Smart Match toggling between AI search and Boolean; reviews say its index includes GitHub activity, patents, publications. hireEZ reviews: crawls "800M+ profiles across 45+ platforms, including LinkedIn, GitHub, Indeed." Gem: "tell your Gem who you're looking for – in your own words"; searches "800M+ profiles, past candidates in your ATS and CRM." Juicebox/PeopleGPT officially says only that it uses "publicly available profile data" from "a variety of compliant sources"; directories claim GitHub, Stack Overflow, technical websites, papers. — [SeekOut AI Search help](https://support.seekout.com/en/collections/14283710-ai-search); [Gem AI Sourcing](https://app.gem.com/product/ai-sourcing); [Juicebox data sources](https://peoplegpt.super.site/data-sources); [hireEZ AI sourcing](https://hireez.com/blog/ai-sourcing/)

### Inferences
- The structured fields (current title, Skills, location, industry, education, dates) are what both Boolean filters and the "coarse" embedding dimensions key on; mismatches between headline and the current Experience title read as noise to humans and may split the embedding signal.
- Because Hiring Assistant learns from recruiter view/contact/advance actions within a role, a profile that converts well on human scan (headline + current title + first lines of About) gets reinforced within that recruiter's search. Human-readability is an AI-ranking input, indirectly.
- Third-party tools aggregate beyond LinkedIn (GitHub, papers, personal sites), so off-LinkedIn proof-of-work plausibly matters more there than in LinkedIn Recruiter itself.

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn documentation on the relative weight of headline vs About vs experience text vs Skills vs recommendations vs Featured vs activity. Recommendations and Featured section are not mentioned anywhere in LinkedIn's search documentation found.
- No documentation on whether Open-to-Work "job titles" preferences feed the ranking model (vs just Spotlight filtering).
- Third-party tools' indexing of personal websites is asserted only by directories, not vendor docs.

## Key Question 3: "AI-written" detection — telltales, evidence of penalty, and what human-written senior copy looks like

### Takeaway
There is no evidence of an algorithmic penalty in recruiter *search* for AI-sounding profile text. There is now verified evidence that LinkedIn's *feed* actively suppresses "AI slop" (classifiers, July 2026 "Seems like AI slop" report button, ~40% fewer views of slop-classified content, private "inauthentic" nudges, removal of its own "enhance your post" rewriter). The penalty on profiles is human and reputational: surveys show a sizable minority of hiring managers say they reject AI-written application material, and recruiters say generic copy reads as low-effort. Telltales are well catalogued (Wikipedia's "Signs of AI writing" is the most rigorous list); the fix is specificity, not punctuation surgery.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn CPO Hari Srinivasan (July 30-31, 2026 post, as reported): LinkedIn added a "Seems like AI slop" report option in the three-dot menu; is "ramping up a series of new and improved classifiers that identify if a post is AI-slop or generally low-quality content," reducing "the amount of AI slop you might see in suggested content and content from outside your network"; "AI and slop are not the same thing"; judgment "should draw on real people's feedback, not depend entirely on an AI detector"; testing private feedback for authors whose posts "seem inauthentic or heavily AI-reliant"; >1 million members used the button in first weeks; views of slop-classified content down ~40% over a few weeks, attributed to classifiers not the button; LinkedIn is removing its "enhance your post" rewrite feature and replacing it with proofreading that "corrects wording without changing the author's voice." — [PureAI, Aug 28, 2026](https://pureai.com/articles/2026/08/28/linkedin-wants-you-to-use-ai-without-sounding-like-ai.aspx); [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/linkedin-cracks-down-automated-content-new-seems-ai-slop-detection-button/1966416); [PetaPixel, Jul 31, 2026](https://petapixel.com/2026/07/31/seems-like-ai-slop-button-added-to-linkedin/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Laura Lorenzetti (LinkedIn VP, exec editor) reportedly said early testing shows the classifier correctly identifies generic content 94% of the time (company-reported, unverified). Earlier LinkedIn had already reduced reach of "engagement bait, recycled thought leadership, and generic content lacking authenticity or originality." — [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/linkedin-cracks-down-automated-content-new-seems-ai-slop-detection-button/1966416); [WorkItDaily newsletter](https://newsletter.workitdaily.com/p/linkedin-is-cracking-down-on-ai-generated-content)
- **[VERIFIED-ish, detector-vendor data]** Pangram estimated 41% of long-form and 30% of short-form LinkedIn posts are likely AI-generated; Originality.AI earlier reported >54% of long-form English posts showed signs of AI generation. Detector-based estimates, not ground truth. — [Campaign](https://campaignlive.com/article/linkedin-cracks-down-automated-content-new-seems-ai-slop-detection-button/1966416)
- **[VERIFIED]** Wikipedia "Signs of AI writing" (WikiProject AI Cleanup field guide) catalogs: overused vocabulary ("delve," "tapestry," "testament," "pivotal," "crucial," "showcasing," "vibrant," "intricate") noting the list shifts by LLM era; copula avoidance ("serves as/stands as/marks" instead of "is"; "boasts/features/offers" instead of "has"); negative parallelisms ("Not just X, but also Y"; "Not X, but Y"; reversed "Y rather than X," "particularly common in recent LLM output"); inflated significance ("is a testament," "underscores its importance"); promotional language ("vibrant," "rich," "nestled"); superficial participial tails ("highlighting/underscoring/emphasizing..."); vague attributions ("Experts argue," "Industry reports"); formulaic conclusions ("Despite its... challenges" then upbeat outlook); "LLMs overuse the rule of three" and "often use this structure to make superficial analyses appear more comprehensive"; "Overuse of boldface" in "an excessive, mechanical manner"; title-case headings; em dashes used "more frequently than most editors do, especially in places where human authors are much more likely to use parentheses or commas"; emoji, markdown artifacts, chatbot residue ("I hope this helps"). Caveats: list is "descriptive, not prescriptive"; "Not all text featuring these indicators is AI-generated"; fixing surface signs "could just make detection harder"; "Humans are notoriously bad at distinguishing human and LLM-generated text"; detectors "have non-trivial error rates." — [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Hiration "Is AI writing your LinkedIn profile and posts hurting you?" (2026, cites 2026 Originality.AI analysis): most-named tell is "It's not X, it's Y" ("It's not about the tools, it's about the mindset"); em-dash *density* ("a dash in nearly every sentence" with no personal detail), not a single dash; announcement openers ("I'm thrilled to announce," "Humbled and honored to share"); buzzwords without data ("Shift. Reshape. Disrupt. Synergy."); "Perfect flatness is its own signature now"; one-idea-per-line cadence; forced parables; emoji bullets. Recruiter reaction: "couldn't be bothered," "low effort"; heavy posting plus obvious AI text "signals outsourced voice," worse than not posting. States "no reliable public evidence" LinkedIn buries posts via an AI-detection filter; generic content fades for lack of saves/replies/dwell. Human-copy guidance: start from "one real datapoint, one honest opinion, and one experience from doing the work"; draft in own words, then use AI only as editor ("make this 20% shorter without losing my voice"); replace "significant impact" with a figure; keep natural rhythm; read aloud — "If it sounds like a press release, it isn't done." — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/ai-slop-linkedin/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Em-dash discourse: Rolling Stone covered a viral clip calling the em dash "the ChatGPT hyphen" and readers assuming brand ad copy was machine-written because it contained two em dashes; TechRadar argues the em dash is no longer a reliable giveaway. Lilach Bullock: removing dashes "doesn't fix a distinctive rhythm underneath the punctuation." — [Rolling Stone](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-features/chatgpt-hypen-em-dash-ai-writing-1235314945/); [TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/ai-platforms-assistants/chatgpt/the-days-of-the-em-dash-being-a-chatgpt-giveaway-are-over-its-time-to-bring-it-back); [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/does-ai-writing-use-a-lot-of-dashes/)
- **[VERIFIED survey, 2025]** TopResume survey of 600 US hiring managers: nearly 20% (19.6%) would reject a candidate who used an AI-generated resume or cover letter; 14.5% think candidates should not use AI at all when applying. Stated intent, not observed behavior; pre-2026. — [TopResume](https://topresume.com/career-advice/ai-in-hiring-survey); [US Chamber summary](https://dev.uschamber.com/co/run/human-resources/hiring-ai-job-applications)
- **[PRACTITIONER, secondary]** 2026 coverage repeats vendor surveys: Resume Now "62% of employers reject AI-generated resumes lacking customization"; Resume.io "nearly half" auto-dismiss applications judged AI-written; Robert Half 67% say reviewing AI-generated applications has slowed hiring. Methodologies not verified. — [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/why-ai-written-resumes-are-backfiring-on-job-seekers-in-2026/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** AI-detector vendor claim: "The Profile Voice Doesn't Match the Interview Voice is the single biggest tell." Vendor marketing. — [aicheckr](https://www.aicheckr.io/blog/ai-detector-for-linkedin-posts)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn's semantic search relevance policy is explicitly about "experience, skills, and role fit" with fairness reviews; nothing in LinkedIn's search or Hiring Assistant documentation mentions style, tone, or AI-detection as a ranking input. — [LinkedIn AI Transparency](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/ai-transparency/hire)

### Inferences
- Two different systems, two different penalties. Feed distribution (posts/comments) now has a documented AI-slop classifier. Profile search ranking has no documented style signal; the cost of AI-sounding profile copy is that human recruiters discount it, and generic text contains fewer of the concrete qualifications the LLM judge is scoring for. So "generic" hurts twice: less evidence for the model, less credibility for the human.
- Specificity is the single move that addresses every tell at once: named tools, numbers, client types, timeframes, trade-offs. Wikipedia's list is dominated by *vagueness* markers (vague attributions, inflated significance, superficial analyses, formulaic structure), not punctuation.
- Senior-practitioner copy by contrast: first person, plain verbs ("is," "built," "ran"), concrete nouns (systems, teams, dollar/percent figures), occasional specific opinion, uneven sentence length, no triads-by-default, no "not X but Y" scaffolding, no closing "In a world where..." line.

### Gaps
- No Paul Graham essay on AI-writing tells was found by search (his Oct 2024 "Writes and Write-Nots" is about writing ability, not detection); no Wired/Verge piece specific to recruiter reactions surfaced.
- No controlled study of recruiters' response rates to AI-sounding vs human-sounding *LinkedIn profiles* (as opposed to resumes/cover letters) was found.
- LinkedIn has not published how the slop classifier works or whether it touches profile text (About/Experience) at all; the documented scope is posts and comments.

## Key Question 4: Proof-of-work signals and structuring a personal site for LLM agents and AI search

### Takeaway
LinkedIn's own agent is described (by its CPO and spokesperson) as looking for "evidence behind a skill" and "specific proof points" — projects, patents, credentials — over titles and schools. One open-sourced LLM screening agent (HackerRank's hiring-agent, June 2026) weights open-source and self-projects at 65% of the score, but also showed score variance of 66-99 on the same resume and was trivially manipulable by hidden text — a cautionary data point about how crude agentic screeners can be. For the personal site: Google documents that no special files or schema are needed for AI features (only indexable, crawlable, textual content); Google's John Mueller says no AI system uses llms.txt; and the strongest large-sample evidence (Semrush, 337k URLs) says cited pages differ most on clarity/summarization, E-E-A-T signals, Q&A format, section structure, and structured data. Build plain, indexable, specific HTML case studies; add Person/ProfilePage JSON-LD with consistent sameAs links; treat llms.txt as optional.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn spokesperson: Hiring Assistant can "understand and evaluate the evidence behind a skill by looking at both explicit and implicit skills"; aims to "find people based on their skills as opposed to traditional proxies like where someone worked or went to school"; candidates should "connect your skills to the work you've done" and "demonstrate skill proficiency by earning credentials." — [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/10/29/linkedin-has-unveiled-a-new-ai-agent-and-ai-job-coaching-tool-heres-how-to-boost-your-cv-f)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** SocialTalent/Srinivasan episode summary: recruiters see "specific proof points" — a patent, a PhD, or a project. — [SocialTalent](https://www.socialtalent.com/podcasts/bonus-episode-linkedins-hari-srinivasan-on-ai-hiring-assistant-the-future-of-recruiting)
- **[VERIFIED, with caveats]** HackerRank's open-sourced `hiring-agent` (late June 2026; Python CLI; PyMuPDF → LLM extraction → GitHub enrichment selecting seven projects by commit activity → second LLM pass rating four categories): rubric gives `open_source` + `self_projects` a combined 65%, `production` + `technical_skills` the remaining 35%. ### Inferences
- For LinkedIn's own agent, proof lives *on the profile*: Experience bullets that name the system built, the metric moved, and the client type; Skills tied to those entries; credentials. External links are not described as inputs to Hiring Assistant; third-party aggregators are more likely to pull GitHub/site content.
- For client inbound via ChatGPT/Perplexity/AI Overviews, the Semrush and Ahrefs data favor short, sectioned, question-answering pages with explicit numbers and author credentials — i.e., a "What I do / Who for / Proof" page with per-case-study H2s, a one-paragraph summary at the top of each, and named outcomes. Google says schema is optional for AI features; ProfilePage/Person JSON-LD is still cheap entity hygiene and harmless.
- llms.txt: no evidence of use by any major AI system; low-cost but do not expect it to move anything. Blocking AI crawlers in robots.txt would be the real mistake.
- HackerRank's agent suggests agentic screeners can over-reward public repos and are noisy; a consultant whose work is closed-source should surface sanitized artifacts (architecture write-ups, redacted dashboards, open-source utilities) rather than assume the narrative suffices.

### Gaps
- No published weights for GitHub/portfolio/writing/talks/client logos in LinkedIn Recruiter or in SeekOut/hireEZ/Gem/Juicebox.
- No evidence on whether LinkedIn's Featured section or Recommendations feed the semantic model.
- No study specific to *personal/consultant* sites being cited by AI answer engines; the citation studies are general web content.

## Key Question 5: Does posting/commenting activity affect recruiter search ranking, or only inbound?

### Takeaway
No LinkedIn documentation ties a member's posting activity to their rank in recruiter search. LinkedIn's documented levers are keyword/skill match, completeness, connection degree, Spotlights (including "engaged with your talent brand"), and the searcher's own behavior. Activity affects the feed (where the AI-slop classifier now operates), the "engaged with company content" Spotlight, and the human impression that a profile is current. Practitioner claims that "the 2026 algorithm prioritizes active profiles" in search are unsourced.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn's listed relevance factors are about the *searcher* (their activity, profile, connections, history) plus "profiles that match the query" and patterns from similar searches; no member-activity factor is named. — [LinkedIn Help a524188](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a524188/)
- **[VERIFIED]** Search Appearances counts "how many people found you through a search on LinkedIn" with searchers' companies and titles; the doc doesn't claim posting changes ranking. — [LinkedIn Help a553050](https://www.linkedin.com/help/learning/answer/a553050); [LinkedIn 2017 launch post](https://news.linkedin.com/2017/6/discover-how-you-re-being-found-in-linkedin-search-with-new-sear)
- **[VERIFIED]** Recruiter Spotlights include candidates who "engaged with their content" (company talent brand), alongside Open to Work and company connections. — [LinkedIn Recruiter overview](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/resources/talent-acquisition/how-to-use-linkedin-recruiter); [Careerflow summary](https://www.careerflow.ai/blog/how-recruiters-use-linkedin)
- **[VERIFIED]** arXiv People Search paper: the *engagement* teacher model uses member text features and the searcher representation includes "professional content, search activity, and historical queries" — this is about the searcher's side and engagement prediction, not a member-activity boost. — [arXiv 2602.07309](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.07309v1)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Multiple 2026 career blogs assert "Recruiter Search ranks profiles by keyword density in your headline and current role, recency of activity, completeness, and 'intent signals' like Open To Work" (unsourced). — [Unilink](https://www.unilink.us/blog/linkedin-job-search-2026); [ResumeGenius](https://resumegeni.com/blog/linkedin-job-search-settings-optimization)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** "Profile appearances" (feed reactions, notifications) is a broader metric than "search appearances"; commenting on high-reach posts pushes the former up without guaranteeing recruiter views. — [Lilach Bullock](https://www.lilachbullock.com/what-do-linkedin-profile-appearances-measure/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Hiration: heavy posting is "a mild yellow flag" to recruiters when combined with obvious AI text. — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/ai-slop-linkedin/)

### Inferences
- Posting is best modeled as an *inbound* and *credibility* channel (people and clients see you; recruiters who click see recent activity), not a search-rank lever. The one documented search-adjacent effect is the "engaged with your content" Spotlight at specific target companies, which rewards engaging with those companies' posts, not posting in general.
- With the feed slop classifier live, low-quality AI posts are now actively down-distributed; the downside of posting badly is larger in late 2026 than the upside of posting generically.

### Gaps
- No controlled evidence either way on member posting frequency vs recruiter search appearances; LinkedIn warns self-testing is unreliable.

## Key Question 6: Cadence — update frequency, "recently updated" ranking, toggling Open-to-Work

### Takeaway
No LinkedIn documentation supports a "recently updated profile ranks higher" effect; the claim is coaching folklore. Open to Work is documented to feed a Recruiter Spotlight filter and LinkedIn's own 2020 data claimed public-frame users were 40% more likely to receive recruiter InMails; the recruiters-only setting is visible in Recruiter without the frame. LinkedIn auto-removes Open to Work if you stop answering InMails. The "looks desperate" view is practitioner opinion with no outcome data; the claim that third-party AI rankers penalize long-running Open-to-Work is a single blog's theory with no evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn Help on Open to Work: collects job titles, location types, locations, start date, employment types; visibility options All members (adds #OpenToWork frame), Recruiters only (Recruiter users), Only you; specifying job types and preferred location helps your profile "show up in search results" when recruiters search; LinkedIn tries to hide status from Recruiter users at your current company but "we can't guarantee complete privacy"; "If you stop responding to recruiter InMails, LinkedIn emails you to confirm... If you don't confirm, the feature is removed automatically." — [LinkedIn Help a507508](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work?lang=en)
- **[VERIFIED]** Recruiter side: after a search, "Open to work" appears under Spotlights; result cards carry an "Open to work" label showing shared details on hover; recruiters-only candidates appear in the spotlight without the frame. — [Recruiter Help a419131](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a419131/view-candidates-who-are-open-to-work-in-recruiter)
- **[VERIFIED, LinkedIn self-reported, DATED]** LinkedIn blog Oct 29, 2020: public #OpenToWork frame users "are, on average, 40% more likely to receive InMails from recruiters" and 20% more likely to receive messages from their community. Correlational, self-reported. Reiterated to Fortune in 2024 with 200M using the feature, 40M publicly. — [LinkedIn Blog 2020](https://blog.linkedin.com/2020/october/29/the-benefits-of-sharing-with-your-community-you-re-open-to-work); [Fortune, Sep 2024](https://fortune.com/2024/09/05/linkedin-opentowork-badge-recruiters-job-search)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Fortune 2024: recruiters/career experts said the public banner can make candidates look "desperate" and invite lowball offers. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/09/05/linkedin-opentowork-badge-recruiters-job-search)
- **[PRACTITIONER, unverified theory]** CareerHack: third-party relevance models (LinkedIn Recruiter, SeekOut, hireEZ, Gem) "trained on hiring outcomes will quietly down-rank signals associated with worse outcomes, and 'openly searching for months' is one of those signals." No data provided. — [CareerHack](https://careerhack.scalis.ai/p/the-open-to-work-banner-is-quietly-costing-you-interviews)
- **[PRACTITIONER, unverified]** A 2026 guide attributes to a "LinkedIn study" a 14.5% vs 4.6% positive response rate for outreach to banner vs non-banner candidates; no link to the study. — [The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=11176)
- **[VERIFIED]** LinkedIn's relevance docs list no "last updated" factor; a help article says "we can't guarantee a particular order." Freshness-boost claims (e.g., "change your headline every two weeks") come from coaching blogs with anecdotal view spikes. — [LinkedIn Help a524188](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a524188/); [WorkItDaily](https://www.workitdaily.com/best-linkedin-tip)
- **[VERIFIED]** Hiring Assistant and ATS-connected evaluation use "up-to-date profile information from both platforms"; spokesperson says it is "even more important for job seekers to keep their resume and profile up to date... with skills and experiences." — [LinkedIn training](https://training.talent.linkedin.com/review-applicants-with-hiring-assistant); [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2024/10/29/linkedin-has-unveiled-a-new-ai-agent-and-ai-job-coaching-tool-heres-how-to-boost-your-cv-f)

### Inferences
- Update when something true changed (new engagement, new metric, new skill), not on a calendar. Profile embeddings are precomputed offline across 1B+ profiles, so edits propagate on LinkedIn's refresh schedule regardless of how often you touch the profile.
- For a consultant who wants client inbound and selective recruiter inbound, "Recruiters only" Open to Work with specific titles/locations/employment types (contract + full-time) gets the Spotlight benefit without the public frame's perception cost. Answer recruiter InMails (even declines) or LinkedIn will silently switch the feature off.

### Gaps
- No evidence on whether toggling Open to Work off/on resets anything or produces a temporary boost.
- No independent (non-LinkedIn) measurement of Open to Work's effect on InMail volume post-2020.

## Key Question 7: Fractional / consultant / founder profiles vs full-time employee profiles under agentic screeners

### Takeaway
No documented case or vendor statement was found about LinkedIn's Hiring Assistant (or SeekOut/hireEZ/Gem) specifically mis-parsing multi-client consultants. The mechanism of risk is well supported indirectly: parsers and LLMs compute "years of experience" and seniority from dated entries, overlapping concurrent engagements can be double-counted or flagged as "dual employment," and LLMs are unreliable at summing overlapping durations. Practitioner guidance converges on one umbrella entry per practice with clients as sub-bullets, a conventional searchable title, and an explicit total-experience statement.

### Cited Findings
- **[VERIFIED]** Hiring Assistant scores "title, seniority, industry, hard skills, and education" from "profile/resume text and structured attributes"; LinkedIn's member help says years of experience filters derive from work-history dates and recommends standard titles. — [LinkedIn Engineering Blog](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/ai/semantic-search-for-ai-agents-at-scale-retrieval-and-ranking-for-linkedins-hiring-assistant); [LinkedIn Help a521944](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a521944)
- **[VERIFIED, vendor docs]** Screening-tool documentation: years-of-experience is an estimate, "not a self-reported field," and "gaps, overlapping roles, freelance projects, or unusual formatting" can affect accuracy. — [GetOnBoard help](https://getonbrd.com/help/how-are-years-of-experience-calculated)
- **[PRACTITIONER, technical]** LLM-CV-analysis guide: models are bad at adding durations that overlap; extract dates and merge overlapping intervals in code. — [bestllmfor.com](https://bestllmfor.com/guide/analyse-cv-automatique-llm/)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** A recruiting-tool vendor's timeline validation flags overlaps as needing investigation because it "could suggest dual employment or misreported dates." — [Peoplebox](https://www.peoplebox.ai/?p=83834)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Freelance-resume guidance: ATS parse freelance work most reliably as one role with sub-bullets for notable clients; the entry needs an employer-name field (business name or "Self-Employed") or it misparses; use one date format; state total years in the summary. — [ResumeGenius](https://resumegeni.com/blog/list-freelance-work-on-resume); [Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=6332)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Careery (Feb 7, 2026) for fractional execs: headline structure "[Seniority + Specialty] | [What You Build/Do] | [Key Impact or Skill]" (example "Fractional CMO | B2B SaaS Growth Strategy | Scaled 5 Companies Past $10M ARR"); "Management Consultant" without specialty "signals nothing about what problems you actually solve"; recruiters "don't type 'consultants' into LinkedIn"; searchable terms include Fractional CMO/CTO/CFO, Independent consultant, Strategic advisor, Interim executive; "Pick 2-3 industries maximum"; use 5-7 keywords consistently. — [Careery](https://careery.pro/blog/personal-brand-keywords-for-fractional-executives)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** Schoenaerts: recruiters check that the headline and most recent Experience title agree; mismatch reads "as noise, or worse, as inflation"; "Head of" and "Lead" may not register in seniority filters. — [Job Search Unlocked](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/linkedin-optimization-the-headline)
- **[PRACTITIONER]** "Most recruiting professionals exclusively use the current title box to search." — [colorado.edu](https://www.colorado.edu/hr/media/2177)

### Inferences
- A consultant's "current title" is the single most exposed field: it must be a conventional, filterable title (e.g., "Director, Revenue Operations" or "Fractional Head of RevOps" is riskier than "Fractional RevOps Director") attached to a real company entry (the practice), because Boolean, seniority filters, and the coarse embedding dimensions all read it.
- Represent concurrent clients inside one Experience entry (the practice) with client sub-bullets, each with a sector, system, and metric; add separate entries only for long single-client engagements with real titles. This keeps date math clean for parsers and keeps the semantic layer fed with specifics.
- Founder/consultant profiles likely under-index on "hard skills" tags relative to employees if Skills are left generic; explicitly tagging tools (HubSpot, Salesforce, Clay, n8n, SQL, etc.) and linking them to experience entries addresses the "explicit skills" path.

### Gaps
- No public statement from LinkedIn or any sourcing vendor on how overlapping Experience entries are handled in seniority/years filters or in MUSE embeddings.
- No documented case of a consultant profile being mis-scored by Hiring Assistant; the risk is inferred from parser behavior and LLM arithmetic limitations.
- No evidence on how agentic screeners treat the "Self-employed" company entity versus a named LLC.
