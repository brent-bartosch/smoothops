# LinkedIn retrieval mechanics: why dropping the exact string "GTM Engineer" collapses inbound, and how to keep retrieval while reading senior

Researched 2026-11-02. State of play Oct–Nov 2026. Confidence labels: **Documented** (LinkedIn's own help/product/engineering pages), **Practitioner** (named recruiter/sourcer testimony, usually un-replicated), **Vendor** (commercial blog with a product to sell, treat as folklore unless corroborated), **Inference** (mine).

Headline finding up front: LinkedIn Recruiter's Job titles filter is a literal match against the title text of Experience entries (current and/or past), and the Keywords filter is a full-profile text search that includes the headline. Neither is documented to stem "Engineering" to "Engineer". The Seniority filter is inferred by LinkedIn from title and company size, not self-reported. Open to Work titles feed a separate recruiter "Open to work" spotlight and the job-recommendation / Hiring Assistant "candidate interest" signals. So "Director GTM Engineering & Revenue Operations" + "Founder & Principal, GTM Engineering" plausibly (a) drops out of every literal `"GTM Engineer"` title query, (b) gets re-bucketed by the inferred-seniority model into Director/Owner, which many recruiters exclude when filling IC/lead seats, and (c) if OTW titles were changed at the same time, drops out of the spotlight and interest signals too. The fix is to keep the exact bigram "GTM Engineer" in the current-position title and in the headline (ideally as the first words), keep "GTM Engineer" in the OTW title list alongside the senior titles, and carry seniority in the surrounding words rather than by replacing the noun.

## Key question 1: How LinkedIn Recruiter matches title and keyword queries (exact vs stemmed, headline vs title field vs standardized title, Boolean, seniority)

### Takeaway
The documented picture is: Job titles filter = Boolean-capable, quotes allowed, matches the title text of Experience positions (toggle Current / Current or past); Keywords filter = Boolean-capable full-profile text search including headline; Seniority filter = inferred by LinkedIn from title + company size. No LinkedIn source documents stemming, so "GTM Engineering" should be assumed NOT to satisfy a quoted `"GTM Engineer"` query; the un-quoted pair `GTM Engineer` would match on "GTM" AND "Engineer" tokens, which "Engineering" also does not supply.

### Cited Findings
- **Documented.** LinkedIn Recruiter help: the filters that accept Boolean are "Job titles, Location, Companies, Skills and Assessments, Schools, Industries, and Spoken languages"; quoted searches and Boolean modifiers can be used "in the Job titles, Companies, or Keywords filters"; the Project filter does not support Boolean. The article gives no stemming or partial-word rule. — [LinkedIn Recruiter Help a415295](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295)
- **Documented.** LinkedIn Community product tip (Aug 20, 2024) confirms the same Boolean-capable filter list and that each filter dropdown offers "Can have (OR), Must have (AND), Doesn't have (NOT)". No statement on synonyms or standardized vs typed titles. — [LinkedIn Community: Apply Boolean Logic in the new Search Filter Experience](https://tcommunity.linkedin.com/product-tips-190/apply-boolean-logic-in-the-new-search-filter-experience-800)
- **Documented (LinkedIn engineer quoted by practitioner).** Boolean Strings (Irina Shamaeva) quoting LinkedIn Engineering's "Dan", Apr 19, 2023: Recruiter has a title taxonomy with gaps ("we don't have 'Jira Administrator' in our taxonomy"); "we let you do Boolean to cover the first 85% of use cases well, but then allow keyword to let you get to 100% coverage"; "it is really (really) expensive to build Boolean functionality outside of keyword searches"; "The 'Current Or Past' just tells us where to look." — [Boolean Strings: LinkedIn Engineering Compromises](https://booleanstrings.com/2023/04/19/linkedin-engineering-compromises/)
- **Practitioner (dated, 2017).** Boolean Strings, Sep 28, 2017: standardized titles carry numeric IDs; selecting a standard title from the dropdown "will pull out ONLY the profiles where the title is exactly 'manager' (no more words)"; "Don't use the selection of standard titles – if you do, it can throw away up to 90% of matching results"; typing the title with a trailing space "switches the internal search to not use the IDs and search by the keywords instead." The author notes the discrepancy "stopped happening" two days later. Flag: 2017 mechanics, LinkedIn has since rebuilt the filter UI (2024) — the underlying taxonomy-vs-text dual path is corroborated by the 2023 LinkedIn engineer quote but the 90% figure is stale. — [Boolean Strings: The Opposite Bug in LIR](https://booleanstrings.com/?p=206372)
- **Vendor (Leonar, Feb 10 2026, updated Sep 3 2026).** Job titles filter "searches the title field of current and past positions," with a "Current" / "Current or past" toggle; matching "is literal" — "Product Manager" will not match "PM" or "Product Lead," so recruiters must add variants themselves or use Boolean in Keywords. The Keywords filter "searches the entire profile, including the headline, summary, experience, skills, and education." No separate headline filter exists in Recruiter. — [Leonar: LinkedIn Recruiter search filters](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Vendor (Leonar, same page).** Seniority filter (Corporate tier only) levels: Entry, Senior, Manager, Director, VP, CXO, Owner/Partner. It is "Inferred by LinkedIn's algorithm, not self-reported," from signals "such as title and company size," and "inferred seniority can be wrong, especially for non-standard titles." — [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Vendor (SlateGit, Jul 31 2026).** Claims the Current Title field is "structured data pretending to be a text box," matched against LinkedIn's internal title taxonomy; a title outside the taxonomy is "Not ranked lower. Excluded from the result set entirely"; "Experience entries feed the same taxonomy matching as your Current Title"; headline is a 220-char free-text line "people read"; semantic search in 2026 "sits on top of the structured filters rather than replacing them." No evidence beyond unnamed "industry write-ups." — [SlateGit: title filter / career changers](https://slategit.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-title-filter-career-changers-invisible)
- **Practitioner (Kristof Schoenaerts, retained-search headhunter, Aug 20 2026).** "A recruiter's search string starts with a job title." "LinkedIn Recruiter matches that string against your headline" and "The title line in each experience entry is indexed"; the headline + current-title pair is "the most heavily weighted pair of fields on your profile"; "Recent titles weigh far more than older ones, and your current entry weighs the most of all." Evidence is "I run searches in LinkedIn Recruiter every day" plus an undisclosed 1,750-profile dataset. — [Job Search Unlocked: The Job Titles](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/linkedin-optimization-the-job-titles)
- **Practitioner (Schoenaerts, Jun 5 2026, paywalled teaser).** "Your title sits in three separate fields, and we match none of them" — headline, experience title, Open to Work titles are matched independently; a headline set "one level up" from the actual title can fail to rank for either title; the "first word of your headline decides whether our search ever finds you." — [Job Search Unlocked: One headline mistake](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/one-headline-mistake-invisible-to)
- **Documented (LinkedIn Engineering, Jan 21 2026, Fedor Borisyuk et al.).** The rebuilt search stack (embedding-based retrieval + LLM judges + small-model reranker) serves **AI Job Search and AI-powered People Search**; a query-understanding layer extracts "title, company, school, and location" attributes that "feed directly into retrieval and ranking"; ambiguous queries route to semantic interpretation, "precise name and entity lookups" route to keyword retrieval. **The post does not mention Recruiter.** — [LinkedIn Engineering: Reimagining LinkedIn's search stack](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/search/reimagining-linkedins-search-stack); secondary coverage [StartupHub.ai, May 22 2026](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/technology/2026/linkedin-s-ai-search-upgrade)
- **Documented (LinkedIn product).** Recruiter's AI-Assisted Search (2024 onward) lets recruiters describe the hire in plain language and "can also create and modify Boolean strings"; LinkedIn self-reports +18% InMail acceptance vs manual-filter sessions. — [LinkedIn: AI-Assisted Search and Projects](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/ai-assisted-search-and-projects); [LinkedIn Community product update Aug 2024](https://tcommunity.linkedin.com/product-updates/ai-assisted-search-projects-august-2024-959)
- **Vendor/aggregator, re stemming.** A 2026 Boolean guide: quoting a multi-word title keeps words in sequence so "Machine Learning Engineer" excludes generic engineer profiles; stop-words like "and/in/with" are dropped from quoted phrases; quoting a single common word sometimes returns MORE results than unquoted. No source anywhere in this research documents LinkedIn stemming "engineer" ⇄ "engineering". — [linkedapi.io Boolean guide](https://linkedapi.io/guides/linkedin-boolean-search); [Boolean Strings on LinkedIn job search quirks](https://booleanstrings.com/?p=211103)

### Inferences
- **"GTM Engineering" does not satisfy `"GTM Engineer"`.** Nothing in LinkedIn's help documents stemming, every practitioner describes title matching as literal, and the 2023 LinkedIn engineer quote describes a taxonomy-ID path plus a keyword path, neither of which is described as stemming. A quoted `"GTM Engineer"` fails on "Engineering" because the phrase token "Engineer" is not present; an unquoted `GTM Engineer` in the Job titles filter is an AND on tokens and "Engineering" is a different token. Confidence: high that it fails under the literal path; medium that AI-Assisted Search / Hiring Assistant semantic retrieval would bridge it (see Q2).
- **Two of the three fields were changed at once.** The old config had "GTM Engineer" in headline (free text, hit by Keywords) and in current-position title (hit by Job titles filter and by the taxonomy path). The new strings remove the bigram from both. Under Schoenaerts' "three separate fields" model, that is zero of three fields still matching the query most recruiters type.
- **Seniority re-bucketing is a second, independent loss.** The Seniority filter is inferred from title + company size. "Director …" at a small company and "Founder & Principal" at a one-person firm are likely to be inferred as Director / Owner-Partner. Recruiters filling a "Senior GTM Engineer" or "Lead GTM Engineer" IC seat commonly set Seniority = Senior or Manager and exclude Director/VP/Owner (practitioner convention, see Q4). Previously, "GTM Engineer" at a consulting shop probably inferred as Senior or Entry, which is inside those filters.
- **Taxonomy gap.** "GTM Engineer" is a 2024–2026 title; whether it is in LinkedIn's standardized-title taxonomy is unknown. If it is, "Director GTM Engineering & Revenue Operations" is an off-taxonomy string, which under the SlateGit/2017-Boolean-Strings model means the structured-title path returns nothing for it. If it is not, both old and new strings rely on the keyword path, and the loss is purely literal-string.

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn documentation on stemming, lemmatization, or synonym expansion inside the Recruiter Job titles filter. Not resolvable without a live Recruiter test: search `"GTM Engineer"` Current title and check whether a known "GTM Engineering" profile appears.
- Whether "GTM Engineer" is a standardized title in LinkedIn's taxonomy (typeahead acceptance is the only external tell; see Q3 gaps).
- The exact weighting of headline vs current title in Recruiter ranking: vendors disagree (most say headline highest; Interview Guys says current title). No LinkedIn source. — [linkedinoptimization](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/11-headline-mistakes-that-hide-you); [The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=4345)
- Whether the Jan 2026 semantic stack has reached Recruiter search (vs. only consumer People Search and Job Search) is undocumented as of this research.

## Key question 2: Which LinkedIn surfaces generate "tapped on the shoulder" inbound, and what each keys on

### Takeaway
Five distinct surfaces, with different inputs: (1) Recruiter manual search → Job titles filter (position title text) + Keywords (full profile incl. headline) + Seniority (inferred) + Spotlights (Open to work, Active talent, More likely to respond); (2) Recruiter AI-Assisted Search → natural-language → structured attributes/Boolean; (3) Hiring Assistant (v1 2025, v2 rolling out "early November" 2026) → up-to-35 qualifications derived from the JD, searched semantically with "candidate interest" (Open to Work, InMail responsiveness, engagement) used for ordering; (4) Jobs side → OTW titles drive job recommendations and the "recruiters look for suitable candidates" matching; (5) Sales Navigator / basic People Search → Current job title field and keyword/headline text, and since Jan 2026 the LLM-based People Search stack. Recruiter manual search is the one that keys most literally on the exact string.

### Cited Findings
- **Documented.** Open to Work help: with recruiter-visible OTW set and job types + preferred location specified, "we'll help your profile show up in search results when recruiters look for suitable job candidates"; "Visible only to you" affects recommendations, not recruiter search. — [LinkedIn Help a507508](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508)
- **Documented.** Job recommendations "reflect information you share with LinkedIn in Open to Work," including job titles, locations, location types, employment types. — [LinkedIn Help 11783](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/11783)
- **Documented (LinkedIn product page, © 2026).** Hiring Assistant "runs dozens of searches across LinkedIn," goes "beyond standard keywords or filters," "learns from your feedback"; Hiring Assistant 2 adds "new LinkedIn fit signals that reflect how you and your company hire," "will begin rolling out to customers in early November" (year not stated on page; context is 2026). LinkedIn-reported stats (Jan 2026 data): "review 81% fewer profiles," "66% higher InMail acceptance rates," "find interview-quality applicants 33% faster." — [LinkedIn: Hiring Assistant](https://business.linkedin.com/hire/hiring-assistant)
- **Vendor summary of LinkedIn material (HeroHunt, 2026).** Hiring Assistant 2 "searches based on what you mean"; intake is converted into "up to 35 qualifications, split into required and preferred"; evaluation uses profile, work history, verified employer, connected-app evidence, pre-screen answers, ATS records; since May 2026 "professional sources" (GitHub, patents) feed evaluation; "Candidate interest" is separate from fit and "estimates how receptive someone is likely to be," using platform engagement, "Open to Work details and InMail responsiveness"; LinkedIn's advice is to use interest to order outreach, not exclude; recruiters are "4x more likely to contact a candidate sourced by Hiring Assistant"; members with a connected app "receive up to 2.9x more recruiter messages." — [HeroHunt: Hiring Assistant 2 (2026)](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/linkedin-hiring-assistant-2-2026-features-cost-limits/)
- **Vendor (Noon.ai, 2026) vs others.** Noon says Hiring Assistant "does not run an autonomous sourcing workflow" but handles steps (search, screening, messaging); Pin notes it "only surfaces people with active LinkedIn profiles" and that pilot stats are company-supplied. — [Noon.ai](https://www.noon.ai/blog/articles/09-linkedin-recruiter-ai-features-2026); [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-new-features/)
- **Secondary (InfoQ / StartupHub).** Hiring Assistant pipeline: a proprietary LLM turns the recruiter's natural-language query into structured role details and qualifications; an LLM "guard" evaluates each candidate's fit and generates natural-language explanations. — [InfoQ presentation](https://infoq.com/presentations/LinkedIn-agent-hiring-assistant); [StartupHub: Hiring Assistant gets smarter](https://www.startuphub.ai/ai-news/technology/2026/linkedin-s-ai-hiring-assistant-gets-smarter.md)
- **Vendor (Leonar).** Recruiter Spotlights: Open to work ("highest-conversion spotlight," visible to recruiters even when hidden from network), Active talent, More likely to respond, Past applicants, Company followers, Internal candidates — "engagement signals, not profile data." — [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Vendor (Sales Navigator workaround).** Founders/sales teams without Recruiter use Sales Navigator's Current Job Title filter and keyword strings, e.g. `"Open to Work" OR ((looking OR seeking) AND (work OR job OR opportunity OR opportunities))`. — [folk.app](https://folk.app/linkedin-how-tos/how-to-find-people-open-to-work-on-linkedin); [lemlist](https://lemlist.com/blog/find-people-open-to-work-linkedin)
- **Documented.** Consumer People Search and Job Search moved to the LLM/EBR stack in Jan 2026, with "precise name and entity lookups" still routed to keyword retrieval. — [LinkedIn Engineering](https://www.linkedin.com/blog/engineering/search/reimagining-linkedins-search-stack)

### Inferences
- **Recruiter manual title/keyword search is the surface most sensitive to the exact bigram**, and it is still the default workflow for most sourcers (LinkedIn's AI features are add-ons at the Corporate tier). This is the likeliest origin of the "GTM Engineer" InMail flood of 2025–2026 given the title's novelty and the 200%+ posting growth (Q4).
- **Hiring Assistant and AI-Assisted Search are semantic and would likely map "GTM Engineering" to "GTM Engineer"**, but (a) they are gated to Corporate/enterprise seats, (b) HA2 is only rolling out in November 2026, and (c) HA's "candidate interest" signal reads Open to Work details, so if the OTW title list was changed, the interest ordering also shifts.
- **Founder / small-company "people search" inbound** (the second big source of GTM-engineer shoulder-taps, since many GTM-engineer buyers are founders without Recruiter) now goes through the Jan 2026 semantic People Search stack; this surface probably degrades less from the string change, but its query-understanding layer extracts "title" as a structured attribute, and a title string missing the noun still weakens it.

### Gaps
- No public documentation of how Hiring Assistant weights Open to Work titles vs headline vs experience titles.
- No data on what share of recruiter InMails originate from manual Boolean search vs AI-Assisted Search vs Hiring Assistant in 2026.
- No source states whether Recruiter's manual Job titles filter was moved onto the EBR stack.

## Key question 3: Open to Work — standardized title list, matching to recruiter searches and recommended-candidate feeds, effect of dropping "GTM Engineer" from OTW titles, 2026 typeahead acceptance

### Takeaway
LinkedIn documents that OTW job titles (plus location/job type) are what make a profile "show up in search results when recruiters look for suitable job candidates" and what drive job recommendations; recruiters see OTW as a Spotlight and Hiring Assistant uses OTW details for "candidate interest." Practitioners report that OTW is a separate field that is matched independently of headline and experience title, and that loading all five OTW slots with step-up titles makes executives invisible. Dropping "GTM Engineer" from OTW while keeping it in the headline therefore likely removes the profile from OTW-driven recommended-candidate and interest feeds even though literal keyword search still finds it.

### Cited Findings
- **Documented.** OTW visibility options: "All LinkedIn Members" (adds #OpenToWork frame, includes current-company people), "Recruiters only" ("People using LinkedIn Recruiter only," privacy not guaranteed vs current employer), "Visible only to you" (job recommendations only). Settings listed: Job titles, Start date, locations, job types. The help page gives no title limit, no typeahead/standardization rule, and no "active vs casual" distinction. — [LinkedIn Help a507508](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508)
- **Vendor.** Up to 5 titles allowed (single-source figure); advice to list variant titles including senior and director versions "add all three." — [resumegeni](https://resumegeni.com/blog/linkedin-job-search-settings-optimization); [resume.co](https://resume.co/blog/how-to-enable-open-to-work-on-linkedin)
- **Practitioner (Schoenaerts, May 24 2026, paywalled teaser).** "Most executives load five title slots with step-ups only, and headhunters never see them"; LinkedIn runs a "'high interest' classifier … against their profile"; filling all five slots with step-up roles makes a profile "invisible in two searches at once." Evidence: "I see it every week." — [Job Search Unlocked: The Open to Work mistake](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/the-open-to-work-mistake-hiding-profiles)
- **Practitioner (Schoenaerts, Aug 2026).** A placeholder current role "overrides your Open to Work signal, because LinkedIn reads it as a new job." — [Job Search Unlocked: Job Titles](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/linkedin-optimization-the-job-titles)
- **Vendor (HeroHunt summarising LinkedIn).** Hiring Assistant "candidate interest" uses "Open to Work details." — [HeroHunt](https://www.herohunt.ai/blog/linkedin-hiring-assistant-2-2026-features-cost-limits/)
- **Vendor (Leonar).** Open to work is the "highest-conversion spotlight" in Recruiter. — [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Vendor (SlateGit, Jul 26 2026).** Advises against turning OTW off during a title change; says OTW "is not the cause" of a post-edit views drop. — [SlateGit views-drop post](https://slategit.com/blog/linkedin-profile-views-drop-after-title-change-explained)

### Inferences
- The "high interest" classifier Schoenaerts describes is consistent with LinkedIn's documented Hiring Assistant "candidate interest" and Recruiter Spotlights ("Active talent", "More likely to respond"). OTW with "GTM Engineer" in the title list, plus recent InMail replies, is what put the profile at the top of those feeds. Replacing OTW titles with only Director/Head strings leaves the interest signal intact but changes which searches the OTW match fires for — exactly the "step-ups only" failure Schoenaerts describes.
- Keeping "GTM Engineer" in the headline but not in OTW titles would preserve Keywords-filter retrieval but lose OTW-recommended-candidate retrieval; keeping it in OTW but not in the current-position title would preserve the spotlight but lose Job titles-filter retrieval. All three need the string.
- Typeahead acceptance: LinkedIn's OTW job-title field is a typeahead against the standardized title list (practitioner consensus, undocumented). Whether "GTM Engineer", "Go-to-Market Engineer", "Forward Deployed Engineer" are accepted strings in Nov 2026 cannot be confirmed from the web; only a live check in the profile UI answers it.

### Gaps
- No LinkedIn documentation on the OTW title cap (5 is a single vendor claim), on typeahead/standardization, or on "actively looking vs casually" (that distinction appears in Recruiter's "Active talent" spotlight, not in the OTW help page).
- No evidence either way on whether "GTM Engineer" / "Go-to-Market Engineer" / "Forward Deployed Engineer" are in the standardized typeahead list as of Nov 2026. Needs a manual check in the OTW editor.

## Key question 4: Do recruiters in 2026 search "GTM Engineer" for senior/lead/head seats, or "Head of GTM Engineering" / "GTM Engineering Lead" / "Revenue Operations"?

### Takeaway
Posting data says the overwhelming majority of GTM-engineering seats are titled with the bare noun: in the 30 days to Oct 26 2026, 78% of 251 distinct roles had no level in the title, 6% Lead/Manager, 4% Senior, 4% Founding, 2% Head/Director. Where level appears it is a prefix on the same noun ("Senior GTM Engineer", "Lead - GTM Engineer", "Founding GTM Engineer"); "Head of GTM Engineering" and "Director, GTM Engineering" exist but are ~2% of the market. Recruiters working from these JDs will seed the Job titles filter with "GTM Engineer" and layer Seniority or years-of-experience filters for senior seats. I found no direct recruiter testimony of query strings for senior GTM-engineering seats.

### Cited Findings
- **Data (getaob.com / Blitz postings index, updated Oct 26 2026, LinkedIn + career pages, agencies excluded).** Last 30 days: 251 distinct roles from 332 postings. Level in title: none 195 (78%), Lead/Manager 15 (6%), Senior 11 (4%), Founding 11 (4%), Intern 10 (4%), Head/Director 6 (2%), Junior 3 (1%). Exact strings quoted: "Senior GTM Engineer," "Senior GTM Systems Engineer (Salesforce)," "Sr. Manager, GTM Engineering," "Lead - GTM Engineer," "Manager - RevOps & GTM Engineering," "Founding GTM Engineer," "Founding GTM AI Engineer," "Head of GTM Engineering (Outbound + Paid Ads)," "Director, GTM Engineering." No "Principal" titles observed. 90-day window: 726 postings, 672 companies. — [getaob.com GTM Engineer Jobs tracker](https://getaob.com/gtm-engineer-jobs)
- **Data (GTME Pulse / State of GTM Engineering 2026, n=228 survey + 3,342 postings, March 2026).** Seniority by years: mid-level 55%, junior 20%, senior + lead/staff 25%; "Lead and Staff GTM Engineer" titles appear at companies with 5+ GTM engineers ($180K–$250K+); "senior" labels "borrowed from software engineering," ladder still forming; "Revenue operations" appears only as a hiring-company type, not a title. 205% YoY posting growth 2024→2025; >3,000 open roles March 2026; Dec 2025 was the largest monthly spike, March 2026 ~15% above it. — [GTME Pulse job market 2026](https://gtmepulse.com/insights/job-market-2026/)
- **Data (Revnu, 2026).** 211 postings analysed, median $159K, "role bifurcated." — [Revnu](https://revnu.partners/blog/gtm-engineer-hiring)
- **Data (Updately, 2026).** ~3,000 open GTM engineer roles early 2026. — [Updately](https://updately.ai/blog/gtm-engineer-hiring-outbound-2026)
- **Data (syncgtm, 2026).** Enterprises (Salesforce, HubSpot) have started posting the role, "often blurring it with sales operations." — [syncgtm](https://syncgtm.com/blog/gtm-engineer-jobs)
- **Practitioner (Leonar).** Because title matching is literal, recruiters "need to add variations yourself or use Boolean in Keywords." — [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Vendor (Knowlee, 2026).** Tech recruiters' 2026 Recruiter workflow is still "Boolean strings, filters, AI stack" layered. — [Knowlee](https://www.knowlee.ai/blog/linkedin-recruiter-for-tech-recruiting-2026)

### Inferences
- The query most recruiters type for any GTM-engineering seat, senior or not, is `"GTM Engineer"` (optionally OR `"Go-to-Market Engineer"` OR `"GTM Engineering"`), because the JD title they are filling contains that noun in ~94% of cases. Seniority is applied as a filter, not as a different noun. A profile whose only noun is "GTM Engineering" relies on the recruiter adding that OR-variant, which Leonar's "literal" note says many do not.
- For the ~2% of Head/Director seats, recruiters likely search `"Head of GTM Engineering" OR "Director, GTM Engineering" OR "GTM Engineering Lead"` AND Seniority=Director; the new strings would match those but they are a 50x smaller pool than the bare-noun searches.
- Many "senior" GTM-engineer seats are filled with Seniority = Senior or Manager filters; a profile inferred as Director/Owner is excluded from those.

### Gaps
- No recruiter testimony (SourceCon, Boolean Strings, Recruiting Brainfood, Hung Lee, Glen Cathey, Jan Tegze) specifically on GTM-engineering query strings was found; the role is too new for the sourcing community to have published strings. Searches for the exact phrase plus "InMail" returned only generic headline-advice content.
- No data on what fraction of inbound to a "GTM Engineer" profile came from agency vs in-house vs founder searches.

## Key question 5: Alternative explanations for the collapse (seasonality, OTW settings, edit cooldown, recruiter-only visibility, 2026 function contraction)

### Takeaway
Several confounders are real but none alone explains a collapse coincident with the edit: Q4 recruiter activity drops 20–25% Oct→Dec in vendor datasets (not to zero); 2026 postings are down ~23% US YoY with sales −27% / marketing −30% in Bain's Q1 2026 data (the "−27%" figure in the brief is Bain's sales-function number, not a "head of function" number — no Sept 2026 head-of-function report was found); profile-edit "cold start" dips are vendor folklore with 3–5 day / 2–3 week timelines but no data; LinkedIn documents indexing lag only for external search engines. The string change is the only variable that fits a step-function drop.

### Cited Findings
- **Data (iCIMS, Dec 2025 report covering Nov 2025).** US openings +4%, applications +6%, hires −1%; "Historically, November dips are normal. However, this year's pullback was steeper than usual," attributed partly to structural caution. — [iCIMS](https://www.icims.com/?p=41460)
- **Data (FactoryFix, late 2024, own platform, skilled trades).** "Between October and December, recruiter momentum fell by more than 25 percent, while applications increased by nearly 20 percent." Single-vendor, different sector. — [FactoryFix](https://factoryfix.com/blog/what-the-2024-numbers-tell-us-about-holiday-hiring-in-the-skilled-trades)
- **Data (Bain / Aura, Q1 2026 YoY postings).** US −23%; by function: sales −27%, marketing −30%, strategy & analytics −26%, R&D/design −37%, HR −12%, G&A −10%. Bain attributes most of it to correction from 2022–2025 over-posting, and notes employment is more resilient than postings. — [Bain Snap Chart: Global job market trends](https://www.bain.com/insights/global-job-market-trends/); [Gulf Business, Jun 2026](https://gulfbusiness.com/en/2026/insights/global-job-postings-fall-sharply-in-early-2026-but-employment-holds-steady-reveals-report/)

## Key question 6: Hybrid headline/title patterns that keep "GTM Engineer" as an exact string while reading senior, and whether they work

### Takeaway
There is no controlled evidence for any specific pattern; what exists is the mechanics above plus practitioner rules (first word of the headline matters; headline and current title should carry the same noun; do not set the headline "one level up"; recent/current title weighs most). The configuration that follows from the documented mechanics is: current-position title contains the literal bigram with a seniority prefix (e.g. "Lead GTM Engineer" / "Principal GTM Engineer"), headline opens with the same bigram and then adds the senior/RevOps framing, OTW titles include "GTM Engineer" plus the senior variants, and Skills include "GTM Engineering" / "Revenue Operations" so Skills-filter and semantic surfaces still bridge the "Engineering" form.

### Cited Findings
- **Practitioner (Schoenaerts, Jun 2026).** The first word of the headline affects whether recruiter search finds the profile; a headline "one level up" from the actual title can fail to rank for either. A CFO "with four job titles" tripled recruiter visibility in two weeks (anecdote). — [Job Search Unlocked: headline mistake](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/one-headline-mistake-invisible-to)
- **Practitioner (Schoenaerts, Aug 2026).** Candidates tagged "Engaged in job market" (12x recruiter visibility, method undisclosed) name their function in 2.3 experience entries on average vs 1.7 for others; "60 to 70% of executive profiles carry at least 1 title no recruiter would ever search for." — [Job Search Unlocked: Job Titles](https://linkedinoptimization.substack.com/p/linkedin-optimization-the-job-titles)
- **Vendor.** Mobile preview shows only ~80 characters of the headline; headline max 220 characters. — [autoapplymax](https://www.autoapplymax.com/blog/best-linkedin-headline-formulas-for-recruiters); [SlateGit](https://slategit.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-title-filter-career-changers-invisible)
- **Vendor anecdote.** A search for "Vice President Sales" can underperform against a profile that only says "VP Sales" — exact wording matters. — [The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=4345)
- **Vendor.** Recruiter's Seniority inference uses "title and company size"; non-standard titles are mis-inferred. — [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Documented.** Skills and Assessments is a Boolean-capable Recruiter filter; Keywords searches skills. — [LinkedIn Recruiter Help a415295](https://www.linkedin.com/help/recruiter/answer/a415295); [Leonar](https://www.leonar.app/blog/linkedin-recruiter-search-filters/)
- **Data.** Real senior titles in the market are "Senior GTM Engineer," "Lead - GTM Engineer," "Founding GTM Engineer," "Sr. Manager, GTM Engineering," "Head of GTM Engineering," "Director, GTM Engineering." — [getaob](https://getaob.com/gtm-engineer-jobs)

### Inferences
- **Recommended configuration (derived, not tested):**
  - Current position title: `Lead GTM Engineer` or `Principal GTM Engineer` (retains the bigram for the literal Job titles filter; "Lead"/"Principal" are used in the market; Seniority inference likely lands at Senior/Manager rather than Director/Owner). If the Founder framing must appear, put it in a second concurrent position (`Founder` at Smoothed) rather than in the same title string, so one title stays on-taxonomy.
  - Headline: open with the bigram, then the senior frame within the first ~80 characters, e.g. `GTM Engineer (Lead / Director-level) | Revenue Operations & GTM Engineering for B2B SaaS | Founder, Smoothed`. Avoid putting "Director" as the first word.
  - Open to Work titles (5 slots): `GTM Engineer`, `Senior GTM Engineer`, `Lead GTM Engineer`, `Head of GTM Engineering`, `Director of Revenue Operations` — a mix of the bare noun and step-ups, not step-ups only (Schoenaerts' failure mode).
  - Skills: add both `GTM Engineering` and `Go-to-Market Engineering`, `Revenue Operations`, `Clay`, and the tools that appear in ≥49% of postings (Clay, Apollo, HubSpot per the Q4 trackers) so the Skills filter and semantic surfaces bridge the "Engineering" form.
  - About section: use both "GTM Engineer" and "GTM Engineering" phrasing naturally so Keywords-filter queries for either hit.
- The "Director GTM Engineering & Revenue Operations" and "Founder & Principal, GTM Engineering" strings can be kept as the *About* opening line and as a second position title; they should not be the only current-title noun.
- Hold the configuration ≥3–6 weeks before judging, per the cold-start folklore, and compare against the same weeks' GTM-engineer posting volume from getaob to separate string effects from seasonality.

### Gaps
- No A/B or longitudinal evidence that any specific hybrid headline pattern restores Recruiter retrieval; all practitioner claims are anecdotal.
- No source on how Recruiter's Seniority inference treats "Lead" vs "Principal" vs "Director" at a one-person or small firm; a live check (view own profile in a Recruiter seat or ask a recruiter contact to run the Seniority filter) is the only way to confirm the bucket.
- Whether parentheticals like "(Director-level)" inside the Experience title break taxonomy matching is unknown; safer to keep the Experience title clean and put qualifiers in the headline.
