# 01 — The case FOR leading with the mechanism-teardown angle

**Date:** 2026-11-09. **Role:** advocate for. **Frame:** 00-frame.md.

Claim tags: [DATA] measured or verifiable, [FIRSTHAND] someone's own account, [OPINION] commentary or marketing. "Old" marks a source older than 18 months (before May 2025). Most survey figures below were read from secondary coverage because the primary pages (Edelman, Gartner newsroom) returned 403; that is noted per source.

## The thesis in one paragraph

The angle is two things, and the second is the one that matters: a mechanism, **explained simply**. Balfe's connection-request post is not written for builders. It addresses salespeople and outbound teams who already use Expandi, Heyreach and Dripify, and every term (HTTP request, cookie, 429) is glossed in a clause. It is a buyer-side explainer that happens to be technically true. The argument for leading with it is that Smoothed's buyers are being sold "AI-native GTM" by a crowd they cannot tell apart, they are being judged on AI fluency they do not yet have, and a plain-English account of how the thing works is the one asset that both teaches them and cannot be faked by a relabeled ops consultant.

Source: https://simonbalfe.com/blog/connection-request-101/ (23 May 2026), read directly.

## Criterion 1 — Resonance with the economic buyer

- [DATA] Gartner's CEO survey finds only 15% of CMOs are seen by their CEOs as having the AI savviness needed for 2026; Gartner's own advice to CMOs is to build AI literacy to protect C-suite credibility. https://www.gartner.com/en/marketing/insights/cmos-build-ai-literacy-csuite-credibility/unlocked and https://www.marketingdive.com/news/gartner-cmos-want-ai-transformation-but-few-are-upgrading-their-skills/812450 (2026; survey attribution differs between sources, not verified against the primary).
- [DATA] Gartner 2026 CMO Spend Survey (401 CMOs, Jan–Mar 2026, released 11 May 2026): 15.3% of marketing budget goes to AI, only 30% say they are ready to scale it. https://gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-05-11-gartner-2026-cmo-spend-survey-finds-cmos-allocate-15-point-3-percent-of-marketing-budgets-to-ai-but-only-30-percent-are-ready-to-scale-ai-capabilities
- Inference, mine: a buyer with budget committed, low readiness and a CEO doubting their fluency has a personal reason to read "how this actually works, simply". Outcome claims do not make them smarter in the next board meeting; explainers do.
- [DATA] Edelman–LinkedIn 2024 (about 3,500 respondents, Feb 2024, **old**): 75% of decision-makers and C-suite said thought leadership led them to research a product or service they were not previously considering; 90% are more receptive to outreach from consistent producers. https://www.edelman.com/expertise/business-marketing/2024-b2b-thought-leadership-report (via secondary summary https://blog.passle.net/post/102jorq/what-decision-makers-think-about-thought-leadership-in-2024).
- Honest limit: none of these surveys test mechanism-level content specifically. They show executives act on substantive content; they do not show that a teardown beats an outcome post. There is no number for that.

## Criterion 2 — Buyer inbound versus peer audience

This is the criterion where my side is weakest; see the end. The best available argument is that the "peer" readers are inside the buying group.

- [DATA] Edelman–LinkedIn 2025 (about 1,900–2,000 respondents, mid-2025): 51% of hidden decision-influencers say strong thought leadership helps them convince C-level executives, 52% say it helps them persuade the rest of the buying group; 95% say it makes them more receptive to outreach. Edelman defines hidden buyers as finance, legal, compliance, procurement and operations. https://www.edelman.com/expertise/Business-Marketing/2025-b2b-thought-leadership-report (403 on fetch; figures via https://www.contentgrip.com/edelman-linkedin-thought-leadership-report/ and https://customerthink.com/thought-leadership-or-brand-which-matters-more-to-hidden-buyers/).
- [DATA] LinkedIn B2B Institute with Bain, "The Hidden Buyer Gap": hidden buyers vetoed about half of shortlisted vendors on risk grounds. https://business.linkedin.com/advertise/resources/b2b-institute/b2b-research/trends/the-hidden-buyer-gap (2025, exact date and sample not verified).
- [DATA] 6sense 2025 Buyer Experience Report (Nov 2025): 94% of buyers rank their shortlist before contacting any vendor, contact their first choice first about 80% of the time, and buy from it 77% of the time. https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251112018032/en/ . The decision is largely made on what was read beforehand, by whoever did the reading. In a Series A–B SaaS that reader is often the RevOps lead or the first GTM engineer, who will also be the person asked "is this consultant real?"
- I found no survey measuring how often a CRO or CMO delegates vendor vetting to RevOps. The closest data cuts against over-claiming: in 60% of organisations RevOps does not control its own tech budget (MarketingOps/Demand Metric 2025, https://marketingops.com/is-your-tech-stack-helping-or-hurting-your-revops-in-2025/). Head of RevOps is, however, on the frame's own list of signers.

## Criterion 3 — Differentiation

- [OPINION, vendor] Explorium's hiring guide (updated 16 Oct 2026) says the title is "inflated at small scale" and gives the test for a real practitioner: can they walk through a system end to end including what broke, and name data sources and API endpoints rather than tool brands. https://www.explorium.ai/blog/data-for-gtm/what-gtm-engineering-actually-means-in-2026-for-revops-and-hiring-managers.md
- [DATA, vendor analysis] A 1,000-posting analysis found that at companies under 50 people nearly 90% of "GTM engineer" roles were outbound outreach under a new title. https://bitscale.ai/blogs/gtm-engineer-jobs-report (2026).
- A mechanism teardown is that vetting test, passed in public before the first call. An outcome claim ("CAC down 90%") is exactly what every relabeled outbound agency also posts, and Smoothed cannot attach a client name to it. When logos are unavailable, demonstrated understanding is the remaining proof.
- The principal's background strengthens this rather than weakening it: a former VP of Sales explaining a pipeline mechanism in sales language is rarer than an engineer doing it, and rarer than a sales leader posting outcomes.

## Criterion 4 — Trust and risk signal

- [DATA] TrustRadius 2026 B2B Buying Disconnect (1,862 buyers, 444 vendors, surveyed Jan 2026, released July 2026): 94% of buyers fact-check AI-generated information; vendor marketing collateral ranks last among resources buyers consult. https://www.marketscale.com/industries/business-services/94-of-b2b-buyers-fact-check-ai-research-outputs-and-vendors-are-underestimating-how-far-trust-has-fallen (secondary).
- [OPINION, analyst] Gartner (March 2026) frames AI washing as buyers starting from "I don't believe you"; vague AI claims now read as risk. https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7622129 (abstract only).
- [DATA, vendor-commissioned] Dataiku/Harris Poll of 900 CEOs: 57% warn explainability gaps could trigger a trust or brand crisis. https://vmblog.com/news/78-of-ceos-say-ai-could-cost-them-their-job-and-their-companys-future-finds-dataiku-global-ai-confessions-report/ (2025–2026, directional only).
- [DATA] Wynter, 100 B2B SaaS CMOs (16 June 2025): every CMO willing to consider an unknown vendor called security and compliance checks non-negotiable. https://wynter.com/post/what-actually-gets-you-on-a-cmos-radar
- A teardown that includes the failure modes, limits and guardrails is a risk document a buyer can forward to legal. That is the version of the angle Smoothed should run.
- Genuine weakness, one line: Balfe's actual topic (driving LinkedIn's private API with a session cookie, no mention of terms of service) trips the frame's second dealbreaker. The angle survives only if Smoothed applies it to its own compliant systems and never to evasion mechanics.

## Criterion 5 — Sustainability and fit

- The raw material already exists: the signals pipeline, the 8-hours-to-3-minutes launch, the $81K ad-spend finding. Each is a teardown ("how a signal becomes a qualified lead for under five cents") that needs no client name, which satisfies the third dealbreaker better than case studies do.
- One mechanism yields one long-form piece and several short posts, which fits 3–5 posts a week for one person. No data on this; it is a judgement.
- [FIRSTHAND, self-reported] ColdIQ began as a content business and became an agency when a reader offered to pay $3K a month for implementation; it reports $4M+ ARR added in 2025 and says LinkedIn content "built trust before it built pipeline". https://coldiq.com/blog/how-coldiq-grew-from-an-affiliate-side-project-to-a-65m-arr-agency (2025–2026, unaudited, figures inconsistent across its own pages).
- [FIRSTHAND, self-reported] Growth Engine X: founder teaches Clay and cold-email mechanics free on YouTube; profiles claim 300+ customers served. https://www.clay.com/experts/partner/growth-engine-x and https://outboundsync.com/blog/growth-engine-x-partner-spotlight/ (unverified, conflicting numbers).
- [FIRSTHAND] Refine Labs: Chris Walker landed first clients, marketing leaders at B2B SaaS, through LinkedIn content explaining how demand generation works. https://howtowin.transistor.fm/episodes/chris-walker-on-using-personal-brand-and-linkedin-to-scale-demand-gen-experts-refine-labs (30 Aug 2021, **old**; the page confirms the topic, I could not read the detail).
- Caveat on all three: teach-the-mechanism services firms exist and grew, but their buyers skew to founders and smaller companies, and none publishes an audited split of who signed.

## The three strongest arguments, ranked

1. **It is the only proof that works without client names in a market that distrusts AI claims.** Buyers fact-check, rank vendor collateral last, and are told how to spot a fake GTM engineer: ask them to explain the system. A teardown answers that in advance.
2. **The buyer needs the education personally.** Budget is committed to AI, readiness is low, and CEOs doubt their marketing leaders' AI fluency. A simple explainer is useful to a CMO or CRO in a way an outcome claim is not.
3. **The supposed peer audience sits inside the buying group.** Shortlists are ranked before first contact, and internal influencers use strong content to persuade executives. The RevOps lead who reads the teardown is the one who carries Smoothed's name upstairs.

## The single weakest point in my own case

I found no evidence, in about 15 searches, that a CMO, CRO or VP Sales reads mechanism-level explainers and buys services because of them. Every buyer survey I can cite is about "thought leadership" in general, the Wynter CMO data says shortlists are driven by brand fame (79%) and peer referral rather than content format, and the reference practitioner himself shows no clients, testimonials or pricing. The examples of firms that grew this way are self-reported and likely sold to founders and practitioners first. On criterion 2, the top dealbreaker, my case rests on inference about buying-group dynamics, and that inference would be equally well served by alternative C (buyer-language claim on top, teardown underneath).
