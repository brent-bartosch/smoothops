# Decision frame — mechanism-teardown angle for Smoothed positioning

**Date:** 2026-11-09

DECISION: Should Smoothed lead its public positioning and content (site, LinkedIn posts, blog) with the "here is how the mechanism actually works, explained simply" teardown angle used by Simon Balfe, when the people it needs to win as clients are marketing leaders, sales leaders and RevOps groups rather than builders?

CONTEXT:
- Smoothed (smoothed.io) is a one-principal GTM engineering practice in Los Angeles, running since Dec 2022. The principal designs, governs and operates production AI/agentic systems for B2B SaaS go-to-market teams. Background: seven years of enterprise SaaS quota-carrying sales (Sales Director, VP of Sales, Enterprise AE) before founding; not a software engineer by title.
- Best-fit clients: Series A–B or PE-backed B2B SaaS where AI-native GTM is a priority. The buyer who signs is typically a CMO, CRO, VP Marketing, VP Sales or Head of RevOps. Engagements are multi-month, scoped and invoiced in phases.
- Proof available: outbound engine (launch time 8 hours to 3 minutes, CAC down ~90%, 5x volume), signals pipeline (~1,100 signals/day, under $0.05 per qualified lead), $81K/year of misdirected ad spend found, 240,000+ retailers sourced at 72% email discovery, customer-risk prediction for a PE-backed SaaS. Current clients cannot be named.
- The reference angle: simonbalfe.com/blog/connection-request-101/ ("How a LinkedIn Connection Request Actually Gets Automated", May 2026). It traces one LinkedIn connection request from the Connect button down to the HTTP request (Voyager API endpoint, URN, li_at cookie, CSRF token, 200/429/403 responses), in plain English with jargon glossed. The author describes himself as a "GTM engineer", is a software engineer at Darktrace, sells freelance/agency work (AI lead gen, agents, MVPs), shows no client logos, no testimonials, no pricing. His other posts follow the same pattern: "How Exa finds the right page in billions, explained simply", "How to scrape any page on the web", "The Argument That Made Me Quit MCPs (Mostly)".
- Market backdrop (Oct–Nov 2026): the "GTM Engineer" title is crowded; many people claim it. Smoothed needs client inbound, not peer followers.
- Capacity: one person. Whatever is chosen has to be sustainable at roughly 3–5 LinkedIn posts a week plus occasional long-form.

CRITERIA (ranked):
1. Resonance with the economic buyer: does a CMO / CRO / VP Sales / Head of RevOps read it, understand it, and move toward a conversation?
2. Buyer inbound versus peer audience: does it attract people who can sign an engagement, or mostly other builders and GTM engineers?
3. Differentiation in a market flooded with "GTM engineer" claims.
4. Trust and risk signal for a buyer handing over revenue systems, customer data and budget.
5. Sustainability for one person, and fit with a principal whose credibility is sales leadership plus operating production systems.

DEALBREAKERS:
- Positioning that draws mostly builders, job-seekers and tool vendors and few people with budget.
- Content that reads as a how-to for platform-terms violations or that would make a risk-conscious buyer (PE-backed, compliance-aware) uneasy.
- Anything that requires naming current clients.

WOULD CHANGE MY MIND:
- Evidence that non-technical B2B buyers (marketing, sales, RevOps leadership) do engage with and buy from mechanism-level technical explainers, with examples of practitioners or firms who won those buyers this way.
- Evidence that they do not: that this content format reliably builds a practitioner audience and sells courses, tools or jobs rather than services to executives.
- Data on what content and positioning B2B services buyers in marketing, sales and RevOps actually respond to in 2025–2026.

KNOWN ALTERNATIVES:
- B. Outcome-led positioning: lead with the business result and the buyer's problem in the buyer's language (pipeline, CAC, forecast, churn), with mechanism kept as supporting proof.
- C. Layered: buyer-language claim on top, mechanism teardown underneath as the evidence that the claim is real ("show the work").
- D. Split by channel or audience: mechanism teardowns for the builder/peer channel, outcome and case-study content for the buyer channel.
